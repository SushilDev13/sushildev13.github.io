01Axion Ray·Lead Analytics·2022–2026

A $60k pilot became a $3M strategic account.

Cummins came to Axion Ray as a pilot. I helped turn it into a $3M ARR strategic account by making one thing undeniable: the quality losses were already sitting in their data.

  1. Problem

    Warranty claims, work orders and service escalations lived in separate systems, written by different people in different words. One recurring engine failure looked like hundreds of unrelated tickets.

  2. First-principles insight

    The losses weren’t hidden. They were fragmented.

    A failure pattern only exists once the same failure is connected across claims, repairs and escalations. The job wasn’t a better dashboard. It was a better join.

  3. What I built

    Analytics workflows that link recurring failure patterns across sources, and LLM pipelines that replaced most of the manual ETL behind failure-code extraction. Around it, the delivery engine: a 24-member team with standardised workflows.

  4. Outcome

    $15M+ in potential quality losses surfaced for engineering to prioritise, 95%+ SLA adherence, and an account that grew from a $60k pilot to $3M ARR.

Implementation notes

Failure-code extraction

LLM-powered pipelines turned free-text failure descriptions into consistent, structured codes, removing roughly 90% of the manual ETL effort and improving consistency across sources.

Product analytics

Funnel analyses, WAU/MAU reporting and feature-flag experiments across an enterprise AI platform used by 450+ client stakeholders, feeding product and operations decisions.

02Publyq·Founder & builder·Live, updated daily

Chennai complains in public. Publyq makes it add up.

Every day, residents report overflowing bins and dead streetlights to the Greater Chennai Corporation, in public. On its own, each complaint is a dead end. Publyq turns them into a map of what is failing, where, and who is responsible for fixing it.

Illustrative record. The structure and the pipeline are real.

  1. 01 / Collect

    It starts as a post.

    Residents raise issues on X. The Corporation replies with a complaint ID. Publyq collects those IDs every day.

  2. 02 / Read

    Then a record with no address.

    Each ID opens the Corporation’s own grievance record: department, complaint type, free-text location. About 58% arrive without a ward, so they can’t be counted against anyone.

  3. 03 / Place

    Then a place.

    Area, locality and street are matched against 3,484 known locations mapped to official ward boundaries. Every record leaves with a ward; zone and constituency follow.

  4. 04 / Own

    Finally, an owner.

    Each record gets one of 18 categories, safety, repeat and overdue flags, English and Tamil text, and the people responsible: the MLA, the zonal officer and the department.

58 of every 100 records arrive with no ward. After Publyq, 100 of 100 have one.

  1. Problem

    The Corporation answers public complaints on X with an ID. The records are public, but each lives alone, and most arrive without a ward. Nobody can be held to account for a pattern nobody can see.

  2. First-principles insight

    Accountability is geographic.

    A complaint becomes actionable the moment it has a place and an owner. So the hard problem wasn’t the AI layer. It was location resolution.

  3. What I built

    A daily pipeline from public post to accountable record: a location index of 3,484 places mapped to ward boundaries, enrichment into 18 categories with risk flags in English and Tamil, and a ward-level explorer.

  4. Outcome

    10,902 complaints, 100% placed across 200 wards, 15 zones and 23 constituencies. 2,888 repeat issues and 1,191 safety risks made visible, published daily for anyone to explore.

Implementation notes

Deterministic first, models second

Grievance data is highly structured, so PostgreSQL and a structured knowledge layer handle retrieval and spatial enrichment before any language model runs. More effort at ingestion, in exchange for answers that are reliable and explainable.

A locally hosted LLM

Conversational analytics run on a local model to keep latency and cost low and avoid dependence on external APIs. Less raw reasoning than frontier models; far more control over privacy and operating cost.
